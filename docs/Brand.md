# Brand

## Brand Definition

AI Media Factory is an AI-powered video localization platform.
The brand must make that immediately clear: this is where teams turn one source video into multiple language-ready versions with AI-assisted transcription, translation, dubbing, subtitles, review, and export.

## Brand Position

AI Media Factory should be perceived as the professional layer between raw AI generation and publish-ready multilingual media.
It is not a toy creator app and not a generic AI assistant.
It is a production platform for teams that care about localization quality.

## Brand Promise

From one source video, AI Media Factory helps teams produce multilingual video faster while keeping human control over meaning, voice, timing, and release quality.

## Brand Attributes

The brand should feel:

- cinematic,
- precise,
- multilingual,
- production-safe,
- technologically advanced,
- and editorially credible.

## Messaging Principles

- Emotion before information on the first screen.
- Lead with video localization, not generic AI language.
- Say exactly what the product does: transcript, translate, dub, subtitle, review, export.
- Emphasize speed with control, never speed alone.
- Explain that AI accelerates the workflow while humans approve the result.
- Avoid broad claims that could describe any SaaS or any AI tool.

## Core Messaging

### One-Line Description

AI Media Factory is an AI video localization platform for creating translated, dubbed, and subtitled versions of video content at production speed.

### Expanded Description

AI Media Factory helps media teams upload a source video, generate a transcript, localize it into multiple languages, create dubbed voice tracks and subtitles, review quality in one workflow, and export assets that are ready to publish.

## Voice And Tone

### Voice

The voice should be:

- expert,
- direct,
- calm,
- operational,
- and quality-focused.

### Tone Rules

- Speak like a professional media workflow product, not a hype-driven AI startup.
- Be specific about outputs and stages.
- Use confidence without exaggeration.
- Make quality and control feel real.
- Reduce uncertainty when discussing AI behavior, timing, or review status.

## Vocabulary

Preferred language:

- source video
- target language
- transcript
- translation
- dubbing
- subtitles
- review
- approval
- export
- publish-ready

Avoid vague replacements when the exact workflow term is better.

## Visual Direction

### Overall Look

The brand should blend media production confidence with AI precision.
It should feel like a serious tool used by localization operators, content studios, and distribution teams.
The first visual impression should feel premium, quiet, and desirable before it feels informational.

### Color Direction

- Light theme is the primary brand expression.
- Use warm-near-neutral surfaces, sharp dark text, and one restrained accent.
- Accent colors should signal active processing, review status, and approved output.
- Visual mood should suggest studio-grade tooling rather than generic enterprise dashboards.
- Avoid bright startup blues, loud gradients, and conventional SaaS card mosaics.

### Typography Direction

- Use typography that feels sharp, modern, and editorial.
- Titles can carry cinematic confidence.
- Body text and metadata must remain highly legible in dense review screens.
- Headline copy should be memorable, not feature-stuffed.

### Imagery Direction

- Prefer video frames, subtitle overlays, waveform fragments, language grids, and workflow imagery tied to localization.
- Avoid unrelated abstract SaaS illustrations.
- Any brand visuals should reinforce multilingual media transformation.
- On first-impression screens, imagery should be minimal and can be replaced entirely by surface behavior, light, and motion.

## Product Brand Behavior

Inside the product, the brand should show up through:

- clear language-stage labeling,
- confident quality status communication,
- careful handling of review and approval actions,
- and polished export presentation.

## Brand Do And Do Not

Do:

- describe the platform as AI video localization software,
- show professional workflow credibility,
- make multilingual production feel manageable,
- and reinforce trust at every review and export step.

Do not:

- describe the product like a generic collaboration tool,
- rely on empty AI buzzwords,
- make the interface look playful when the task is production-critical,
- or hide workflow complexity behind vague marketing language.
